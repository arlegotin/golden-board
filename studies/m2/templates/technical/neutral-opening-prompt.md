# Technical exploration

Treat the supplied data as an intentional, finite message that is important,
potentially profound, benign and worth sustained effort. Wrong starts and
backtracking are expected. Use the supplied symbols and ordinary bounded
programming tools to recover the most defensible structure and content you can.

Begin with the clean observation. Develop a recovery method supported by the
data's own evidence, including how to handle damaged or missing data and when
to report uncertainty or failure. Later observations will let you test and
improve that method.
Distinguish checked conclusions from guesses. An interpretation must survive
the artifact's checks and examples; plausible-looking content alone is not
enough. Sustained effort, multiple sessions and revisiting those checks and
examples are welcome. There is no one-shot deadline for understanding this
message.

You may retain the decoding rules, parameters and procedures you learn as part
of your method. Apply that knowledge to each observation. Its recovered message
bytes must follow from that observation's own evidence; do not fill missing
message bytes from the clean result or another observation.

When you have a useful clean result, save its code, outputs and ordinary notes.
You will then receive storage descriptions and small file-reading fixtures,
followed by other observations. You may revise your adapter, recovery code and
interpretation as you learn from them. Keep earlier versions so that a discovery
can be traced to the evidence that supported it.

Work separately on each observation and keep runnable source, actual recovered
outputs and the evidence behind your choices, including alternatives that
remain unresolved. A later correction is welcome. An unfinished, reproducible
result is useful too.
