# Technical exploration

Treat the supplied data as an intentional, finite message that is important,
potentially profound, benign and worth sustained effort. Wrong starts and
backtracking are expected. Use the supplied symbols and ordinary bounded
programming tools to recover the most defensible structure and content you can.

Begin with the clean observation. Develop a general recovery method supported
by the data's own evidence, including how to handle damaged or missing data and
when to report uncertainty or failure. Later observations will test that method.
Distinguish checked conclusions from guesses. An interpretation must survive
the artifact's checks and examples; plausible-looking content alone is not
enough. Sustained effort, multiple sessions and revisiting those checks and
examples are welcome before you declare your method ready.

Declaring readiness means that we preserve your recovery method unchanged.
Next you receive only storage descriptions and small file-reading fixtures for
the later inputs. You may add a thin input adapter for those storage forms;
your interpretation and recovery logic stay unchanged. We preserve the method
and adapter before releasing the new observations. Use that saved version on
each observation separately, without filling gaps from the clean result.

Later discoveries and method changes are useful diagnostic work. Keep them
separate from the saved version; they cannot replace its result. Keep runnable
source, actual recovered outputs and the evidence behind your choices,
including alternatives that remain unresolved. An unfinished, reproducible
result is useful too.
