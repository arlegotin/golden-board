# Sixty-Four Masterpieces of Chess

## A complete chronological anthology of beautiful, elegant, inspiring and educational games

> **Scope.** Sixty-four games played under the normal rules of chess, from 1834 to 2024. Every entry contains the players, the best available date and event information, all additional metadata preserved in the selected score, an exact critical FEN, an educational explanation and the complete main-line game score in PGN.

## How to read this anthology

- **Critical FEN** is the exact six-field position immediately *before* the listed signature move. It was regenerated by replaying the complete score, not copied manually.
- The full scores are clean main lines. Source comments and side variations were removed so each PGN can be pasted directly into a normal chess viewer.
- `!` and `!!` in the signature move are editorial judgments used by the anthology; they are not part of Standard Algebraic Notation.
- In historical PGNs, `??` means that the corresponding month or day was not recorded. The anthology preserves that uncertainty rather than inventing a date.
- Source event and site strings are retained in each record when they differ from the normalized, reader-friendly identification.

# Editorial and validation notes

## Selection and interpretation

Beauty in chess is not objectively rankable. This anthology is therefore a curated canon rather than a claim that no other set of sixty-four games is defensible. The selection aims to cover direct attack, positional restriction, defense, counterattack, endgame geometry, passed pawns, sacrifices, quiet moves, opening preparation, technical conversion and competitive resilience across nearly two centuries.

The Opera Game was played by Morphy against two consulting opponents, but it is one ordinary legal game. Draws are included deliberately: creative defense and dynamically balanced play are part of chess beauty. Rapid chess is represented once, in the 2016 World Championship tiebreak; the rules of play were still normal chess.

## Score handling

The selected main line of every game was parsed and replayed with a legal-move chess library. Player names, events and sites were normalized for readability while raw source fields were retained in the individual records whenever they differed. No missing historical date component was guessed. The PGN score itself contains no engine analysis and no source commentary; it is the complete recorded main line.

## FEN convention

Every critical FEN contains piece placement, side to move, castling rights, en-passant target, halfmove clock and fullmove number. The en-passant field follows strict FEN semantics, including a target square when the preceding double pawn move created one even if no legal en-passant capture is available.

## Provenance

Scores were consolidated from machine-readable historical, player and event PGN collections. Each game’s record names the selected collection and source filename. The anthology’s explanations, organization, normalized metadata and critical-position verification are original editorial work.
